The question: does metal music cause anger, or do angry people use metal to process it? There's a meaningful difference, and most of the existing research conflates the two. This paper synthesizes three empirical studies to sort it out. The finding that holds across all of them: metal doesn't create anger, it processes it. Fans deliberately seek it out during emotional distress, much like others reach for sad music during grief. The most direct evidence comes from Olsen et al. (2022) — the top emotions reported by metal fans listening to metal are empowerment and joy, not aggression. The one study suggesting negative effects (Chundakal et al., 2021) used elderly Indian participants with no familiarity with the genre, which says more about cultural conditioning than the music itself.
